## Why this source is here

This is the central source for the repository because it treats Young tableaux as one mathematical object seen from several sides rather than as decorative notation attached to a representation-theory chapter.

The book is organized around three connected layers:

1. tableau combinatorics — bumping, sliding, words, RSK, Littlewood–Richardson, symmetric polynomials;
2. representation theory — the symmetric group and the general linear group;
3. geometry — flag varieties, Schubert varieties, and Schubert polynomials.

For the present project, Chapter 7 (“Representations of the symmetric group”) is the most direct bridge from tableaux/tabloids to representations, but the point of keeping the whole book in view is that the same combinatorics reappears in the later algebra and geometry.

## Credit and thanks

Deep thanks to **William Fulton** for writing the book and to the **London Mathematical Society** and **Cambridge University Press** for publishing and maintaining it.

The preface itself makes the mathematical ancestry unusually visible. The source explicitly foregrounds constructions and ideas associated with **Craige Schensted**, **Marcel-Paul Schützenberger**, **Donald E. Knuth**, **Alain Lascoux**, **Gilbert de Beauregard Robinson**, **D. E. Littlewood**, and **A. R. Richardson**, among others. These names belong in the project’s credit trail because the modern tableau calculus is visibly cumulative work, not a self-contained invention of any one text.

That is historical/mathematical attribution, not a claim that any of those people participated in or endorsed this repository.
