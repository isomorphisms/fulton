## Why this source is here

This is the fourth core book because it is not merely an introduction to the characteristic-zero picture. It treats both ordinary and modular representation theory and therefore shows which familiar tableau/Specht constructions are structural and which simplifications depend on the field.

Its opening chapters make it especially relevant to the present project:

1. symmetric groups and Young subgroups;
2. ordinary irreducible representations and characters;
3. ordinary irreducible matrix representations;

with later chapters moving into modular representations, representations over arbitrary fields, and general linear groups.

## Credit and thanks

Deep thanks to **Gordon D. James** and **Adalbert Kerber** for the book, to **Cambridge University Press** for maintaining the current edition, and to **Addison-Wesley** for the original 1981 publication.

The Cambridge edition also preserves an introduction by **G. de B. Robinson**. In their preface, James and Kerber explicitly situate the early theory in the work of **Ferdinand Georg Frobenius** and **Alfred Young**, and direct readers to **D. E. Rutherford** for an account of Young’s substitutional analysis. Those historical attributions belong in the project’s provenance trail.
