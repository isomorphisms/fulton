# Jeremy Booher — “Representations of the Symmetric Group via Young Tableaux”

## Citation

Jeremy Booher, “Representations of the Symmetric Group via Young Tableaux,” expository notes dated July 31 and August 7, 2011.

- Author/institutional PDF: https://www.math.canterbury.ac.nz/~j.booher/expos/young_promys.pdf

## Why this source is here

Booher’s note is particularly useful because it starts from a concrete representation-theory problem rather than from definitions: after seeing the familiar irreducibles of `S_4` and only part of the character table of `S_5`, the question becomes how to construct the missing irreducibles systematically. Young tableaux are introduced as the principled answer.

The note then moves through tableaux, tabloids, permutation modules, Specht modules, dominance, standard bases, the hook-length formula, and the branching rule. That makes it a good source for explaining not just *what* the machinery is but *why one would invent it*.

### Every work cited in the note

The reference list contains three items:

- **William Fulton** and **Joe Harris** — *Representation Theory: A First Course*.
- **Bruce E. Sagan** — *The Symmetric Group: Representations, Combinatorial Algorithms, and Symmetric Functions*.
- **Yufei Zhao** — “Young Tableaux and the Representations of the Symmetric Group.”

Booher explicitly describes Fulton–Harris as an algebraic route, Sagan as combinatorially focused, and Zhao as a readable survey. Preserving those contrasts will be useful in the later synthesis.

## Pass 2 target

The summary should preserve Booher’s motivating question:

“How do we stop finding irreducible representations of `S_n` ad hoc?”

Then follow the answer through the construction of `M^λ` and `S^λ`, the submodule/dominance arguments, the standard-tableau basis, hook lengths, and branching. The point should be to show the *mechanism* by which a diagram becomes an actual representation.
